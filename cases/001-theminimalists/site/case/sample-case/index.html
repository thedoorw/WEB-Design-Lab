A Poster Built from Three Rules

Synthetic artwork — portrait study

This is placeholder content designed to occupy approximately the same kind of reading space as a finished project note. The purpose is to test the page architecture without borrowing the reference site's editorial content.

The work begins with three constraints: one geometric module, two type sizes, and a fixed margin. Each revision changes only one relationship at a time, allowing the final composition to emerge from a small system rather than a large set of decorations.

Rule One: Keep the module visible

The repeated form should remain legible even when it becomes a larger field. The page therefore alternates between concentrated clusters and quieter areas.

Synthetic process image — 4:3

Rule Two: Let text carry sequence

Typography is used to tell the viewer where to start, where to slow down, and where to stop. The hierarchy is deliberately shallow.

Rule Three: Stop before the page is full

The final version leaves enough unused space to keep the structure visible. Removing one more element often improves the result more than adding another.
